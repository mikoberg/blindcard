# Blindcard

Spoiler-free fight rating site. Fans see which fights on a card are worth watching without learning results.

## Structure
- `/ingest`: Python 3.12 scrapers, scoring, CLI (`pytest` for tests)
- `/web`: Next.js App Router + TypeScript + Tailwind
- `/supabase/migrations`: Postgres schema (Supabase)
- `/ml`: future excitement model

## The spoiler rule (most important)
Public card views must never expose, directly or indirectly: winner, method, round, finish time, fight duration, bonuses (Fight/Performance of the Night), scorecards, or post-fight records.
- Result data lives in `fight_results`, separated by RLS. It's only served through the explicit reveal path.
- This also covers page titles, meta/OG tags, URL slugs, alt text, blurbs, API responses, logs shipped to the client, and sort orders other than rating.
- Explicit exception (judge pages): a judge's most disputed scorecards (max 10, any date) name fights, official results and all three cards. They sit in the private `judge_disputes` table, are served only by `judge_disputed_cards` through a POST-only route, and load only after a click on a button with a warning next to it. They are never part of a page render, and judge pages otherwise show totals only.
- Start times of upcoming events come from the official event page (cited by the Wikipedia article): only three UTC timestamps are read, robots.txt is checked first and its crawl delay (15 s) is honoured, and only events within 45 days are read. Never widen what is read from that page: it holds the card and, after the event, its results.
- Fighting styles (`fighters.style`, shown on the fighter's page, not on the cards) come from the fighter's Wikipedia page (infobox style and belts) and, where that has nothing, one field of the official athlete page, read slowly (robots.txt checked, 15 s crawl delay, at most 40 per run, each fighter marked so they are not asked again for months). Fixed list of labels only. Display only: no prediction uses style.
- Upcoming events (`/upcoming/[slug]`, tables `upcoming_events` / `upcoming_bouts`) hold pre-fight facts only: names, weight class, part of the card. Never read an article on or after its event date, refuse a card that already shows a result, and an upcoming card shows each fighter's record going into the bout (today's record, computed from the going-in record of their latest completed fight plus its result, and left out when either is unknown), exactly as completed cards show the record going in; it shows no streak note, rating of the fight or result. Known and accepted: two consecutive records differ by the result in between.
- Explicit exception (upcoming picks): who is favoured in an upcoming bout is learned from past results, so it sits in the private `upcoming_picks` table, is served only by `upcoming_pick` (one bout per call) through a POST-only route, and loads only after a click on a button. It is never part of a page render, is symmetric in the two fighters, is refused unless a walk-forward test clearly beats a coin, and goes when the bout goes (the day after the event). The expected rating (public data only) is not a result and stays on the page.
- Explicit exception (Elo leaderboard, `/fighters/elo`): an Elo rating is built from who beat whom, so the list says how recent fights went. It sits in the private `fighter_elo` table and is served only by `elo_leaderboard` (active fighters with at least 8 fights, at most 100 rows) through a POST-only route. By product decision there is NO spoiler warning or click gate on this page: the browser asks for the list as soon as the page has opened, so it is still never part of a page render (the HTML holds no fighter and no rating). The calculation behind one fighter's rating (every fight, opponent, how it was decided, expected score, K and change) sits in `fighter_elo_steps`, is served only by `elo_fighter_history` (one fighter per call, at most 120 rows) through a POST-only route, and loads only when that fighter is opened on the Elo page. The page text carries no result and never names a fighter. Never sort or show Elo anywhere else.
- Card finder on the home page (`CardFinder`, `/api/explore`): every card can be ordered and filtered on what is known BEFORE the fights: ratings, the Elo and UFC ranks going in, title fights, five-round fights, rematches, weight classes, countries, places, dates. It comes from the public `event_facets` view (the same pre-fight facts as on the cards, summed per event; never a fight count) and works straight away. There are deliberately NO result-based orders or filters (knockouts, submissions, how long a card ran, upsets): nothing in the finder is built from a result, so there is nothing to lock.
- Elo going into a bout (`fights.elo`, `upcoming_bouts.fighter_*_elo`; today's rating for an announced bout) is shown on the cards next to the record, as the same kind of pre-fight fact: computed from EARLIER fights only (a fight's own result never goes into its own number), never the rating after a fight or a change. Like two consecutive records, two consecutive ratings differ by the result in between; this is known and accepted. A fight with no usable result (a no contest) gets its ratings like any other, so its absence says nothing. Never show a rating after a fight, a change, or the board's peak or history anywhere but the spoiler page.
- Official UFC ranks (`fights.ranks`, `upcoming_bouts.fighter_*_rank`; 0 = champion, 1 to 15) are shown on the cards as the same kind of pre-fight fact as the record going in: for a completed fight the ranking in the last revision of Wikipedia's "UFC rankings" article from BEFORE midnight (UTC) of the event date, for an announced bout the newest one. A rank is read for the fighter in the division of the bout's weight class only, names are matched without guessing, and nothing else is read from the article's history.
- The fighters list (`/fighters`) can also be ordered by career totals of how a fighter's UFC fights in our data ended (`fighters.tally`: victories by knockout, submission and decision, first-round finishes, title fights, knockdowns, strikes, takedowns and submission attempts landed), by product decision and with no warning. Each is one total per fighter as of today (it includes the latest event, the same accepted trade-off as the record), written by `compute-elo`, shown as one column, never fight by fight. Fighters need 8 rated fights to be on the list at all.
- Fighter pages also show how many Fight of the Night and Performance of the Night bonuses a fighter has earned (`fighters.awards`, "FOTN" and "POTN", from 2015 on, the years we hold the awards for; a Fight of the Night counts for both fighters, a Performance of the Night for its winner). Explicit exception, decided by the product owner: it is one career total as of today, of the same kind as the record (it includes the latest event, the same accepted trade-off), written by `ingest-bonuses` from the private labels, never fight by fight and never with a name of a fight. The fighters list (`/fighters`) can be ordered by them (Most Performance of the Night, Most Fight of the Night, most in all) and shows the number as a column. Only a fighter's own page and that list may carry the bonus wording (the live spoiler suite exempts exactly that pattern on `/fighters` and `/fighters/<slug>`); no other page, card or API response may.
- Fighter pages show a fighter's current standing: the record as of today (`fighters.record`) and the Elo as of today (`fighters.elo`), both including their latest fight, like the record and Elo on an announced bout (the same accepted trade-off). Only ever as one current figure, never fight by fight and never with a change: a per-fight record or rating would spell out every result of a career. The rest of the page is public ratings, weight classes and title-fight flags of rated fights.
- Explicit exception (a fighter's results, on the fighter page): who they beat and lost to, and how, for every fight in the list at once. It is result data, so it is served only by `fighter_results` (one fighter per call, at most 120 fights, read from the private tables) through a POST-only route, and loads only after a click on a button with a spoiler warning above it. The same click also lists the rest of the career (earlier fights and other promotions, from the fighter's Wikipedia table, in the private `fighter_bouts` table, served by `fighter_other_bouts`, at most 150 bouts) so that the lists add up to the record shown above them. Two buttons open it, both with the spoiler warning in their own words: the one in the history section and the record at the top of the page ("Full history (spoilers)"). Opened, it is one list of the whole career with how each fight ended and the record the fighter had after it (worked out in the browser from today's record, never stored per fight). It never appears in a page render, and never anywhere but behind those buttons.
- Run the `spoiler-check` skill before finishing any user-facing change.

## Legal guardrails
- Never use "UFC", "Octagon" or UFC logos in branding. Event and fighter names as plain facts are fine.
- No fighter photos or official imagery.
- Footer: "Unofficial fan project, not affiliated with any promotion."
- Scrape politely (~1 req/s, identifying user agent, cache raw HTML) and only what we need.

## Conventions
- Complete files over partial diffs; production-ready code (types, error handling, logging).
- Scoring weights live in config, not in code. Scores are versioned.
- Ask before adding dependencies outside the agreed stack.
- Times are stored in UTC and displayed in the user's timezone (default Europe/Amsterdam).
